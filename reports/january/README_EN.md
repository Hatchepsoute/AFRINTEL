## 📈 Strategic observations

### 🔴 New: massive defacement in Niger
- **January 4, 2026**: Multiple Nigerian government websites defaced simultaneously
- **Common message**: All sites displayed the same politically-charged content
- **Unclaimed**: The attack was not claimed by any known group
- **Vulnerability**: This coordinated attack reveals a common security flaw in government infrastructure
- **Risk**: These defacements, while cosmetic, hide deeper access security issues

### 🔴 Recurrent targeting of Togo by Bigbrother
- **September 2025**: First SSH access claimed on `gouv.tg` put up for sale
- **January 2026**: New access to other government platforms
- **Modus Operandi**: Initial Access Broker specialized in government infrastructures
- **Risk**: Accumulation of access could facilitate future attacks (ransomware, espionage)

### 🔴 Emerging data leaks
- **PixPay (Senegal)** : January 16 - Financial data exposed
- **AOM Aviation (Morocco)** : January 31 - Aviation databases leaked

### 🔴 Major cross-border threat actors
- **thegentlemen** : 6 incidents in 4 countries - **major actor**
- **tengu** : 5 incidents in 5 different countries - **pan-African presence**

## 🛡️ Post-Incident recommendations

### 🔴 Lessons learned from Niger defacement
- **Enhanced monitoring** of West African government infrastructures
- **Security audit** of public websites to detect common vulnerabilities
- **Update** content management systems and servers
- **Threat intelligence** on coordinated attacks targeting multiple sites of the same institution

### 🔴 Lessons learned from Bigbrother and Togo
- **Enhanced monitoring** of IABs (Initial Access Brokers) targeting African governments
- **Credential rotation** and security audits after each claimed access
- **Information sharing** between countries to detect cross-border attack patterns

### 🔴 Lessons learned from data leaks
- **Sensitive sectors** : FinTech (PixPay) and Aviation (AOM) particularly vulnerable
- **Recommended monitoring** : Detection of databases offered for sale on the darkweb
- **Communication** : Rapid user notification in case of leaks

### For all African Governments
1. **Strengthen security** of critical infrastructures (CFM Mozambique, NWA Kenya, government websites)
2. **Audit access** to government databases (Togo, Niger, Witzenberg)
3. **Establish incident response** units (defacements, leaks, ransomware)
4. **Monitor IABs** specifically targeting public administrations

### For the private sector
1. **Monitor active groups** : `thegentlemen` (6 incidents) and `tengu` (5 incidents)
2. **Protect financial data** (PixPay, CPF, Rogers Capital)
3. **Segment networks** to limit ransomware propagation
4. **Enforce MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION (MFA)** on all privileged accounts

## ℹ️ Supplementary note on operational modes

**NB:** Cybercriminal groups adopt different strategies:

- **Ransomware**: Data encryption + ransom demand
- **Data Leaks**: Publication or resale of exfiltrated databases
- **Double extortion**: Encryption + threat of publication
- **Initial Access Brokers (IAB)** : Sale of access to corporate or government networks
- **Defacement**: Website appearance modification for political or ideological purposes, often revealing deeper security flaws
